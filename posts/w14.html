Week 14- Journal Entry

Finishers Wanted

“In life, as in business, there has always been a need for those persons who could be called finishers. Their ranks are few, their opportunities many, their contributions great.”

— Thomas S. Monson

This message by President Thomas S. Monson is such a powerful and inspired reminder about endurance and purpose in our lives. He begins by sharing a simple experience of noticing a sign that read “Finishers Wanted,” and how those words stayed with him and led him to reflect on life. He teaches that life is like a race, and each of us must decide if we will falter or if we will finish. He uses examples from the scriptures to show that even people like King David, the rich young man, and Judas, who started strong, can fall if they lose focus, while others like Job, Paul, and the Savior faced trials and still finished faithfully. He explains that those who finish develop qualities like vision, effort, faith, virtue, courage, and prayer.

As I reflect on this message, I feel encouraged to keep going even when things are hard. I learned that finishing does not mean being perfect, but choosing every day to try again and trust in the Lord. Small choices matter, and they shape who I am becoming. This message helped me see that I need to rely more on prayer and have faith during challenges. I want to become someone who stays committed and does not give up, but continues with patience and trust in God until the end.

Parts 2.3 and 2.4 of Recognizing and Shaping Opportunities

“Entrepreneurs use the discussion of the business model to identify and clarify the assumptions that drive revenues and costs as they forecast cash flow and design experiments to test the validity of their business models.”

— Lynda M. Applegate, Crafting Business Models, Harvard Business School, 2008

This reading was very insightful. It highlights how important it is to shape a business idea with purpose and understanding, not just to have a good idea. It shows that entrepreneurs need to think carefully about how their idea will work by creating a clear strategy, identifying the resources and skills required, and understanding the value they offer to others. It also emphasizes that this process is never perfect from the start. There is always uncertainty, and ideas need to be tested, adjusted, and sometimes even changed. A business grows through learning and adaptation, not just planning.

Reflecting on this, I realize that success comes from patience and a willingness to learn along the way. It is not about getting everything right the first time but about paying attention, making better decisions, and improving step by step. I learned that being flexible and open to change is just as important as having a vision. This reading helped me understand that growth, both in business and in life, happens when we keep trying, learning, and moving forward, even when the path is not completely clear at the beginning.

Acton Hero Tom Monaghan

"Right now my life is so right, for the way God made me. I believe I'm doing exactly what God wants me to do and I feel so privileged that he gave me the wisdom to see it."

— Tom Monaghan

This is such a great video, especially because my kids love Domino’s Pizza, so it felt really personal to watch. I loved seeing Tom Monaghan’s journey, from starting with a single pizzeria to creating a delivery empire, and how he combined business success with faith, humility, and generosity. It was inspiring to see that even someone who achieved so much recognized the importance of using their talents and resources to make a positive impact on others. True success of course can result in money, but what really stood out was that real fulfillment comes from giving, making a difference in people’s lives, and following a purpose bigger than yourself.

Peak Performance

“Get prepared, because your opportunities will come and if you are not striving to be in ideal performance data at your best, your days of standing on the victory podium will pass to another.”

— Jim Ritchie

This video Peak Performance is such an eye-opener because it reminds us that success doesn’t just happen. Jim Ritchie emphasizes the importance of creating a life plan and a personal constitution to identify the areas in our lives that need attention and to develop the skills and habits we need to perform at our best. He shows that strategies used by professional athletes to stay calm under pressure can apply to anyone, helping us handle challenges and seize opportunities. The key lesson is that by being intentional, building mental toughness, developing leadership and communication skills, and committing to continuous improvement, we position ourselves to reach our full potential and make the most of every opportunity.

Final Entry

As I reflect on the past fourteen weeks, I am struck by how much I have grown, both personally and in my understanding of entrepreneurship. This course has been more than just a series of lessons; it has been a journey of discovery, self-reflection, and inspiration. One of the most significant lessons I have learned is that success is not measured only by profits or achievements, but by the impact we have on others and the integrity we bring to our work. I have learned the importance of finding balance between family and career, and how maintaining that balance enriches both areas of life. I have also realized the value of giving back to the community, cultivating gratitude, and appreciating the blessings and opportunities that come my way.

Many course concepts resonated deeply with me, particularly the importance of service and helping others. Elder Jeffrey R. Holland’s message, Are We Not All Beggars?, alongside examples of extraordinary individuals like Mother Teresa, reminded me that even the smallest acts of kindness matter when done with a sincere heart. The entrepreneurial lessons were equally powerful. Studying leaders like Charles Handy, Muhammad Yunus, Tom Monaghan, and Randy Haykin showed me that entrepreneurship is more than starting a business; it is a mindset of creativity, resilience, and service. I have learned that doing what we love is essential, and when we combine our passions with purpose, we create something of true value to our community. Opportunities exist everywhere, but recognizing them and acting with courage, integrity, and love for others is what turns ideas into meaningful impact.

If I were to give one final lecture to a group of students, I would focus on the idea that personal growth and service must go hand in hand with entrepreneurial ambition. I would encourage students to pursue their passions, take risks, and develop skills, but always to align their work with values, compassion, and purpose. I would share that true success comes when we create value not just for ourselves, but for the people and communities around us, and when we balance our professional ambitions with our relationships and personal wellbeing.

For someone just beginning their entrepreneurial journey, my advice would be to start with observation and preparation. Pay attention to the needs of others, remain open to learning, and do not fear failure. Every challenge is a lesson, and every small step can lead to opportunities beyond what you imagined. At the same time, never compromise your integrity or forget the importance of serving others because your character is the foundation that will sustain your efforts in the long run. Find joy in what you do and recognize how it can positively impact the lives of others.

If I had only one chance to give a last piece of guidance, I would tell aspiring entrepreneurs to embrace balance and intentionality. Value your relationships, nurture your mind and heart, and stay grounded in your principles. Success will not come overnight, but when pursued with faith, diligence, gratitude, and compassion, it will be meaningful and lasting. Remember that entrepreneurship is not just about creating a product or a business; it is about creating value, improving lives, giving back, and growing into the person you are meant to be.

Overall, this course has taught me that the path to success is intertwined with personal development, service, passion, and balance. These lessons will stay with me as I continue to grow both as an individual and as someone seeking to make a positive impact in the world.
